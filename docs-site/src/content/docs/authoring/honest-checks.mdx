A doctor is only worth running if its findings can be trusted. The
contract enforces that with rules, not vibes.

## State the claim, not the conclusion

A check's `claim` is the observable condition it fires on: "A fetch call
whose options contain no signal." Not the conclusion ("this is a bug")
and not the advice ("add AbortController"). The claim is testable — a
fixture can prove it fires and prove the lookalikes stay silent.

This is why a check without a claim cannot pass the verify gate. Impact
prose explains a finding; it cannot substitute for a statement of what
was observed.

## Name the lookalikes

Every shape that resembles the claim but is fine belongs in
`lookalikes`: "signal threaded through a variable the pattern cannot
see." Lookalikes get fixtures too. A doctor that cannot tell the
difference between a mistake and an idiom is noise with confidence.

## Declare what you cannot see

`blindSpots` is a required honesty field. The async doctor does not know
whether a helper supplies a signal at runtime — so it says exactly that,
in the report, next to the findings. Blind spots are not a weakness to
hide; they are the difference between a tool and a sales pitch.

## Decide what happens on the unknown

`onUnknown` tells the runtime what a check does with incomplete
evidence:

- `"narrow"` — report only what is provable; silence is the default.
- `"skip"` — this check never reports on partial evidence.

Both are conservative. There is no `onUnknown: "vibes"`.

## One finding, one unit

`reportingUnit` states what a single finding covers: each `occurrence`,
once per `file`, or once per `project`. Findings never blur into
"various issues in this area."

## Fixtures prove it forever

A doctor ships with `*.fixtures.mjs` files: the shapes it must catch and
the lookalikes it must spare. `any-doctor verify` replays them. Behavior
that is not pinned by a fixture is behavior you cannot claim you have.

The result: findings arrive pre-annotated with their limits, decisions
attach to identities that survive refactors, and a doctor that oversteps
its evidence fails its own gate before it ever wastes your review.
